International research in the classroom: a visit from Dr. Akira Sarodo

yen-ju chen November 03, 2026
academic-exchange international-exchange visual-perception soochow-university

On November 3, 2026, the Department of Psychology at Soochow University welcomed Dr. Akira Sarodo from Ritsumeikan University, Japan, for a seminar on visual adaptation and the perception of space and time. Organized by Yen-Ju Chen, the event was intended primarily for students in the department, bringing an international researcher’s work and approach to scientific communication into their learning environment.

Bringing international research into students’ learning

Dr. Sarodo is a postdoctoral researcher at Ritsumeikan University’s OIC Research Organization. His seminar, “Adaptation to imperceptible motion links size and duration compression,” took place in Room C301 from 10:00 to 11:30. It was delivered in English and included time for questions.

Inviting researchers from overseas gives students direct access to questions being studied in other academic settings. They can hear how a researcher explains an experimental design and interprets evidence. The English seminar and question period also offered a chance to encounter international academic communication and connect their perception courses with ongoing research.

Can perceived size and duration change together?

Space and time are usually treated as distinct dimensions of perception. Visual adaptation studies, however, suggest that some stimuli can make an object appear smaller and an interval seem shorter. Dr. Sarodo used an individual differences approach to ask whether these changes covary.

The study used the same adaptor in size and duration judgments and compared several temporal frequencies. Size compression occurred at all tested frequencies and was weakest at 60 Hz. Duration compression occurred at higher frequencies, including 10, 20 and 60 Hz. At 60 Hz, the two compression effects were significantly correlated.

A second experiment with a larger sample found that 60 Hz adaptation reduced the points of subjective equality (PSEs) for both size and duration relative to a non-adaptation baseline. The two baseline-corrected shifts were positively associated. These findings are consistent with a shared adaptation-related contribution and with possible involvement of the magnocellular visual pathway. Further evidence is needed to establish the underlying mechanism.

Academic exchange on campus

The visit gave Soochow psychology students a chance to hear directly from a researcher based in Japan within their own department. By organizing the seminar, Yen-Ju Chen brought an international academic connection into students’ everyday learning and created an opportunity for exchange between the visitor and the department’s students and faculty.

We thank Dr. Sarodo for visiting and sharing his work, and the students and faculty who joined the seminar.
