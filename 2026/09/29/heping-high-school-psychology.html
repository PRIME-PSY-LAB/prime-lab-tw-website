Introducing Psychology and Soochow University at Heping High School

yen-ju chen September 29, 2026 November 04, 2026
news outreach psychology soochow-university

On September 29, 2026, I visited Taipei Municipal Heping High School to give a talk titled “Introducing Psychology and Soochow University.” We explored what psychology studies, what students learn in a psychology degree, and the study and career directions they might consider. I also introduced the learning environment and resources at Soochow University.

Download the slides (PPTX, Traditional Chinese, approximately 60 MB)

Everyday questions and psychological science

The talk opened with familiar ideas about psychology, such as reading minds, dividing people into “left-brained” and “right-brained” types, and the claim that we use only ten percent of our brains. These ideas provided a starting point for asking how we could examine a claim and what evidence we would need.

Psychology studies behavior and mental processes scientifically. Everyday experiences give us plenty to investigate: how we perceive an image, why we forget something, how an advertisement influences a choice, and why different people respond differently to the same situation. Learning psychology means turning such questions into research that can be tested.

What psychology students learn

I introduced the foundations of a psychology degree, including general psychology, physiology, statistics, psychological experiments, and psychological testing. These subjects help students connect an interest in people with methods for collecting and interpreting evidence.

The Stroop task offered a concrete example. When a word’s meaning conflicts with its printed color, that conflict can affect how we respond. By controlling the task conditions and measuring responses, a psychological experiment makes an otherwise invisible process open to investigation. The section on psychological testing introduced another approach: using standardized procedures to measure and compare psychological characteristics.

The talk also surveyed several fields. Perception concerns how we experience the world through our senses. Cognitive psychology examines attention, memory, learning, and decision-making. Developmental psychology studies change over time, while social and personality psychology examine social influences and individual differences. Together, these examples gave students a broader picture of the questions they could explore at university.

Study choices and career directions

Choosing a department also means thinking about the kind of work one might want to pursue. The slides surveyed psychology and counseling-related departments in Taiwan and introduced career directions in psychological services, education, community support, public service, research, and industry.

Examples included human resources, market research, user experience research, and data analysis. The career diagrams also showed that different routes involve different skills and further training. My aim was to help students consider both what interests them and what they would need to learn along the way.

Studying at Soochow University

The final part introduced Soochow University’s Department of Psychology, including its new dedicated space in 2026 and the Psychology Learning Hub. I also discussed campus resources such as foreign-language study, double majors and minors, teacher education, and microprograms, alongside international exchange and scholarship opportunities presented in the slides.

These examples were an invitation to imagine university life in more concrete terms: which subjects students might combine, what practical learning they could pursue, and how they might broaden their experience through study abroad.

Thank you to the teachers and students at Heping High School for the opportunity to share this introduction. I hope the talk gave students a clearer view of psychology and useful questions to carry into their own decisions about university.
